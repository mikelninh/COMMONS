# COMMONS ACTION 001 — Nepal: Flash Floods 2026

Status: **OPEN LOOP**  
Public experience: `/?action=nepal-flash-floods-2026`  
Evidence review date: **2026-09-19**

## Purpose

This is the first WORLD PULSE Action Card.

It tests whether COMMONS can preserve an accountable chain from:

```text
world signal
→ verified humanitarian need
→ identified responder
→ concrete action channel
→ observed response evidence
→ later outcome updates
```

The card is designed to be compelling enough to share without turning urgency into unsupported certainty.

## Verified facts used in the public card

### Event and estimated impact

IFRC reported that catastrophic flash flooding struck northern Nepal on **26 August 2026**.

On **27 August 2026**, IFRC said:

- around **93,000 people may have been affected** while assessments continued,
- homes were destroyed or badly damaged,
- bridges and roads were damaged, cutting off communities,
- Nepal Red Cross Society teams were delivering clean water, shelter, first aid and emergency supplies.

Primary source:

https://www.ifrc.org/press-release/ifrc-launches-chf-25-million-emergency-appeal-response-devastating-nepal-flash-floods

### Formal response

IFRC launched a **CHF 25 million Emergency Appeal** to support the Nepal Red Cross Society response.

The 24-month appeal covers areas including:

- shelter,
- cash assistance,
- health,
- household relief items,
- water, sanitation and hygiene,
- mental health and psychosocial support,
- restoring family links,
- medium- and longer-term recovery.

IFRC's emergency list still identifies **Nepal: Flash Floods 2026** as an ongoing Emergency Appeal at the evidence review date.

Sources:

https://www.ifrc.org/emergencies/all

https://www.ifrc.org/appeals?type%5B0%5D=24

### Response evidence

In an update published **8 September 2026**, IFRC reported that:

- IFRC and Nepal Red Cross Society were providing emergency shelter, health care, clean water and sanitation, and mental health and psychosocial support;
- water tanks and a water pump in Nuwakot were helping restore safe drinking water access for around **2,000 people**;
- a mobile primary health care clinic could reach **100 patients per day**.

Primary source:

https://www.ifrc.org/press-release/nepal-floods-ifrc-delivers-safe-water-and-health-care-affected-communities

## Claims we deliberately do **not** make

- We do not claim the ~93,000 estimate is a final affected-population count.
- We do not claim every affected person has been reached.
- We do not claim a funding percentage unless a current authoritative source exposes one.
- We do not claim a user's donation directly caused the displayed outcome evidence.
- We do not claim the 2,000 safe-water figure or 100-patient/day clinic capacity measures the overall success of the operation.
- We do not treat the response as complete; the card remains an **OPEN LOOP**.

## Update rule

When a newer authoritative IFRC / Nepal Red Cross source contains measurable outcome evidence, update the Action Card only if the new evidence is:

1. attributable to the identified operation,
2. dated,
3. specific enough to interpret,
4. linked to a primary or first-party humanitarian source,
5. not presented as causal evidence unless causality is actually established.

Historical facts should retain their original date rather than silently being replaced.

## Sharing contract

A shared Action Card should preserve:

- event,
- source organisation,
- affected-population estimate and its uncertainty language,
- appeal amount,
- dated outcome evidence,
- responder,
- donation / participation channel,
- primary evidence URLs,
- COMMONS deep link.

## Success criteria for Action 001

The experience succeeds if a viewer can answer within about one minute:

1. What happened?
2. How do we know?
3. Who is responding?
4. What do people currently need?
5. What evidence shows the response is real?
6. What can I do?
7. Where can I verify every important claim?
8. How will I know what happened next?

The final question is why the loop stays open.
