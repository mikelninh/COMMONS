# COMMONS Food Pilot — Collaboration-First

Food surplus is a useful physical-world pilot because supply, need, transport and
delivery can often be observed within hours.

## Do not rebuild mature networks

Too Good To Go already operates a large surplus-food marketplace and an
enterprise surplus-management platform. Its official materials describe modules
for near-expiry management, marketplace sales and charity redistribution with
traceability.

COMMONS should treat mature systems like Too Good To Go as capabilities inside a
larger network, not automatically compete with them.

Potential future adapter:

- marketplace capability: route saleable consumer surplus to an established channel
- donations capability: route appropriate donations through established charity infrastructure
- proof adapter: ingest authorised transaction / collection / donation receipts
- fallback composition: identify missing transport, language, recipient capacity or other capability

No unofficial scraping or reverse-engineered integration should be required.
Pursue an official business/technology partnership before automating against
their systems.

Official starting points:
- https://www.toogoodtogo.com/en-us/platform
- https://www.toogoodtogo.com/en-gb/become-a-partner

## First independent COMMONS experiment

Before any partnership exists, run a tiny controlled pilot with consenting local
participants:

Supply:
- one food business with confirmed surplus

Need:
- one organisation able to receive it

Missing capability:
- transport, if required

Proof chain:
1. surplus declared
2. recipient capacity confirmed
3. pickup confirmed
4. delivery counterparty-confirmed
5. independent verification where practical
6. costs recorded
7. outcome recorded

The first goal is not scale. It is one auditable chain:

NEED -> CAPABILITY -> AUTHORISED ACTION -> VERIFIED OUTCOME
