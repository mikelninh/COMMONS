# COMMONS Vision

## The problem

Civilisation has extraordinary intelligence but weak conversion of intelligence into coordinated outcomes.

The recurring failure mode is:

```text
information → understanding → decision → coordination → execution → feedback
```

Every arrow leaks.

People cannot find the right knowledge. Experts work in silos. Decisions hide assumptions. Institutions route slowly. AI often stops at advice. Outcomes are rarely measured. Lessons do not reliably flow back into the next decision.

COMMONS exists to close that loop.

---

## Ultimate goal

> **Make trustworthy intelligence and coordinated action as broadly available as information is today.**

A person, community, scientist, organisation or public institution should be able to express a real need in natural language and receive the best available combination of:

- understanding,
- evidence,
- decision support,
- specialist intelligence,
- human expertise,
- tools,
- coordination,
- execution,
- verification,
- and follow-up,

in a form they can actually use.

The system should learn from what happens in reality.

### The human north star

> **Expand people's practical capability to live healthy, free, meaningful and joyful lives.**

COMMONS does not define the good life for people. It should increase their ability to understand, choose, act, create, connect, learn, care and participate.

Human flourishing remains plural and contestable. We therefore do not reduce it to one master score.

---

## What the mature system looks like

Imagine an open intelligence layer connecting:

```text
people
communities
researchers
AI models
specialists
public services
companies
non-profits
sensors
software
robots
institutions
```

Every participant can expose **capabilities**.

Every incoming problem can be decomposed into **decisions**.

Every action is constrained by **policy and permissions**.

Every important prediction carries **uncertainty**.

Every intervention can produce an **outcome record**.

Every outcome can improve future routing and decisions.

This is not one omniscient AI.

It is an **accountable network for coordinating intelligence**.

---

## Five layers

### 1. Sense

Fast judgments over messy state.

Examples:

- What kind of problem is this?
- How urgent is it?
- How risky is automation?
- What capability is required?
- Is evidence sufficient?

Jev is our first System One primitive here.

### 2. Mind

Deep reasoning.

Examples:

- research synthesis
- planning
- scientific hypothesis generation
- explanation
- multi-step problem solving

Different reasoning systems can compete or cooperate.

### 3. Hands

Execution.

Examples:

- APIs
- workflows
- applications
- bookings
- forms
- logistics
- experiments
- eventually robotics

### 4. Memory

Evidence and outcomes.

COMMONS should remember:

- what state was observed,
- what was predicted,
- what decision was made,
- what action occurred,
- what outcome followed,
- what it cost,
- where humans intervened.

### 5. Constitution

Inspectable constraints.

The system's authority, safety rules, thresholds and permissions live outside model weights wherever possible.

Models may advise.

They do not silently grant themselves power.

## Desired properties

A mature COMMONS should be:

**Accessible**  
Low-cost, multilingual and usable by people without technical expertise.

**Pluralistic**  
Different values, institutions and domain rules can coexist without pretending there is one universal objective function.

**Observable**  
Important decisions and actions leave auditable traces.

**Calibrated**  
Confidence means something measurable.

**Able to abstain**  
Uncertainty should cause review, not confident improvisation.

**Recoverable**  
Mistakes should be containable and reversible wherever possible.

**Open**  
Capabilities, policies and outcome schemas should eventually have public interfaces.

**Adaptive**  
The network should improve from real outcomes rather than merely from user engagement.

**Agency-preserving**  
The system should make people more capable, not merely more dependent.

---

## What COMMONS is not

COMMONS is not:

- a chatbot wrapper,
- a single foundation model,
- a social network optimising engagement,
- an oracle that decides human values,
- an autonomous government,
- an excuse to automate high-stakes decisions without accountability.

The purpose is not to remove humans from civilisation.

The purpose is to help humans and machines **coordinate intelligence better**.

---

## The civilisational thesis

If useful intelligence becomes cheap while action remains fragmented, impact will disappoint.

If powerful agents can act while authority is opaque, risk rises.

If decisions are made without outcome feedback, systems repeat mistakes.

COMMONS therefore focuses on the connective tissue:

> **understand → decide → coordinate → act → verify → learn**

If that loop becomes cheap, safe, measurable and widely accessible, improvements in science, education, health, public services, agriculture, logistics, business and many other domains can compound through the same substrate.

That is the bet.
